Community Manager Toolkit

A free plugin for Claude and ChatGPT. It helps community managers introduce members to each other, match people at events, keep one clean member directory and show the value of a community with outcomes instead of activity.

Install for Claude Install for ChatGPT View on GitHub

Built from what worked in a real ~850-member professional network. Free and open source (MIT). No account to create. The skills have no server. The optional connector stores nothing.

Eight skills for community management

Getting started

Shows the eight jobs with a prompt to copy for each, checks which of your tools are connected, and writes a Community profile so no skill asks the same thing twice.

Member directory

One clean member table from your CRM, Slack or Discord, sheets, forms and email tools. De-duplicated, with the gaps listed and a plan to fill them. No phone numbers or email addresses in the directory.

Weekly community review

Up to seven things to do this week, each with the prompt to run it, spaced so no member gets two messages.

Event matchmaking

Pair attendees from a Luma or Eventbrite guest list (or a CSV) with a reason and a backup for everyone. Get a host sheet, “find [name]” messages and a morning-after follow-up.

Member introductions

Consent-first, double opt-in warm introductions. Screens out sales pitches, checks identity when names are similar, and follows up until you know the conversation happened.

New member onboarding

A first month built around one named introduction instead of a feature tour, with day-3, day-14 and day-30 check-ins.

Member re-engagement

Segment quiet and dormant members, pick the right touch for each, and draft a small batch of personal messages instead of a broadcast.

Community impact report

A board or sponsor report built on outcomes: conversations that happened and how members rated them. Data coverage is stated honestly.

Ask in plain words: “What can you do for my community?”, “Match the attendees of my next Luma event” or “Help me introduce two members, double opt-in”.

An optional connector that does the sums

The skills work on their own. Add the connector and seven tools do the exact work in code: mapping, merging, pairing, counting and checking. The answer is the same every time. The assistant keeps the judgement and the writing.

The connector processes what your assistant sends it in memory and stores nothing. There is no sign-in. Address: https://toolkit.commonos.ai/mcp

ToolWhat it does
detect_member_fieldsMaps the columns of any export (CRM, Slack, Discord, forms, email tools, Luma, Eventbrite) to member fields and flags contact columns so they are ignored.
merge_member_recordsMerges records from several sources into one directory. Lists uncertain matches for you instead of merging them. Never returns email addresses or phone numbers.
pair_attendeesPairs event guests, with a backup for each. Never the same organisation, never a pair you have ruled out, a cap on introductions per person.
segment_membersSorts members by activity and says who can be messaged now without breaking your spacing rules.
plan_weekBuilds this week’s list of up to seven items and resolves spacing conflicts so no member gets two proactive messages.
compute_impact_metricsCounts the introduction funnel, outcomes, ratings, event attendance and new-member activation exactly from your ledger.
check_draft_messageChecks a draft against the channel’s rules before you send it: length, markdown, more than one question, placeholders, contact details in a consent ask.

Where your assistant supports it, pairing shows a table you can tick, edit and approve in the chat, and the impact figures show as a report. Otherwise you get the same answer as text. Every tool is read-only: none of them can send a message.

Why it works this way

The rules come from one ~850-member professional network running member introductions over WhatsApp from June to September 2026. Figures are rounded, come from that one network, and are a reference point, not a benchmark.

  • A named person plus a yes/no beats a feature tour. “[Name] asked to meet you, yes or no?” got replies about 63% of the time. A generic event reminder got about 28% and a one-off broadcast to members never contacted before about 18%.
  • Roughly 1 intro request in 3 became a real conversation, and when people did meet they rated it about 4.7 out of 5. You only know that if you log every introduction and ask afterwards.
  • New members who were introduced to a named person in their first days replied about 59% of the time, against about 47% for a plain welcome message.
  • Registration is not attendance. At one event only about 30% of pre-matched pairs had both people turn up, so every guest gets a backup.

Guardrails built in

  • Drafts only. Nothing is sent unless you explicitly ask, one message at a time. Nothing is ever bulk-sent.
  • Consent first. Nobody’s details go to another person until they have said yes.
  • No same-organisation pairs, and identity checks (full name and organisation) when two members have similar names.
  • Professional reasons only. Match reasons never mention or infer sensitive attributes.
  • Data minimisation. Reports, the member directory and the intro ledger never include phone numbers or email addresses.
  • Cooldowns and spacing so members are not over-messaged, and opt-out or removal requests are honoured immediately.

How to install

The toolkit is a set of instructions (skills) that your AI assistant follows, plus the optional connector. Pick your assistant:

Claude (claude.ai, Claude Desktop, Claude Code)

  1. Open the Claude plugin directory. (Directory link coming once the listing is approved.)
  2. Search for “Community Manager Toolkit” and install it.
  3. Optionally add the connector from your connector settings, and connect your CRM, Luma, Notion, Slack or Google Drive.
  4. Type a request such as “Match the attendees of my next Luma event”.

ChatGPT

  1. Open the ChatGPT Plugin Directory. (Directory link coming once the listing is approved.)
  2. Search for “Community Manager Toolkit” and add it.
  3. Optionally connect the tools you use, then start with one of the suggested prompts.

Without installing

The skills are plain Markdown files in the plugin/skills folder on GitHub. You can read them, adapt them, or paste one into any AI assistant.

Works with your tools, or just a file

Everything is optional. The toolkit uses whatever you have connected:

  • CRM: HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, Pipedrive, Affinity, Zoho CRM.
  • Community: Slack (connected) and Discord (upload a member list).
  • Events: Luma, Ticket Tailor, RSVPify, and Eventbrite attendee reports.
  • Sheets and notes: Airtable, Google Sheets, Excel, Notion, Google Drive.
  • Forms and email tools: Typeform, Tally, Google Forms, Jotform, Mailchimp, Kit, beehiiv, MailerLite.
  • Files: any CSV export, a WhatsApp chat export, a LinkedIn connections export.

With nothing connected, upload a CSV export or a WhatsApp chat export and the skills work from the file.

Privacy in short

  • The skills are instructions only. They have no server and collect and store nothing.
  • The optional connector processes what your assistant sends it in memory, then discards it. It stores nothing, logs no content, has no sign-in and runs in London.
  • Your data is also processed by the AI assistant you run the toolkit in, and by any tools you connect, under those providers’ terms.
  • This website uses no analytics and sets no cookies.
  • You are responsible for having a lawful basis and any consent needed for the member data you use (UK GDPR and GDPR).

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. The plugin and the connector are open source under the MIT licence, and the hosted connector is free to use, provided as is. Your AI assistant or the tools you connect may have their own costs.

Does it send messages to my members?

No. It writes drafts. Sending happens only if you explicitly ask it to, through a connected tool that asks you to confirm, or when you copy the text yourself. The connector cannot send anything.

Do I need the connector?

No. Every skill works without it. The connector makes pairing, merging and counting exact and repeatable, and gives you an approval table in the chat where your assistant supports it.

Who is it for?

Anyone running a professional network, association, alumni group, members’ club, accelerator cohort, event series or WhatsApp community, especially small teams where one person does everything.
