# Message craft

How to write every member-facing message this toolkit drafts: the writing rules first,
then the evidence behind them, then ready-to-use templates.

Templates are a starting shape, not a script. Fill every [square-bracket] variable with
real details or cut the phrase. Write in the manager's voice, in the first person, and
match the manager's spelling (UK or US), formality and language.

## Writing rules

1. **Lead with a named person.** Every message names one specific person (or one specific
   event) and says why in a single line. Never "here are some people you could meet".
2. **Make them wanted, not offered.** "[Name] asked to meet you" beats "you could meet
   [name]". Only say someone asked if they really did.
3. **One question, one decision.** End with a single yes/no question or three numbered
   options. No second question and no "also...". An update with nothing to decide ends
   with one clear next step instead.
4. **Report what changed.** When something happened that the member didn't cause (a
   request lapsed, someone said yes, a match is waiting), say so plainly in the first line.
5. **No feature tours.** Don't explain the community, the platform, the benefits or how
   introductions work. One person, one reason, one question.
6. **Short.** Under about 500 characters, one idea per message. Many chat platforms cap
   approved templates at 1,024 characters including variables; stay well under.
7. **Reasons come from work.** A reason line uses professional overlap and stated asks and
   offers only. Never mention or hint at sexuality, religion, ethnicity, health,
   disability, politics or similar, and describe an organisation neutrally when its name
   signals one of these. See `guardrails.md`.
8. **No contact details before a double yes.** A consent ask shares name, role,
   organisation and the reason, nothing more. Contact details go out only in the connection
   message, after both people have said yes.
9. **Personal, never broadcast.** Each message is written for one person. The same text to
   many people is a broadcast, and it counts against the spacing limit in `guardrails.md`.
10. **Give the requester the first move.** Every connection message asks the requester to
    send the first message within a day.
11. **Follow up on a timetable, then stop.** About 3 days after the intro: "have you found a
    time?". About 10 days after: the outcome, with three options. If the answer is "not
    yet" or "didn't connect", one last check a week later. Nothing after that.
12. **Write as the manager.** First person, the manager's name, the community's tone. No
    emojis unless the manager uses them. Never pretend a message is from someone else.

## Why these rules exist

In one ~850-member professional network (a member agent on WhatsApp, June to September
2026; figures rounded), this share of members replied within 7 days, by message type:

| Message | Replied within 7 days |
|---|---|
| "Your intro request lapsed; want me to try someone else?" | ~65% |
| "[Named person] asked to meet you, yes or no?" | ~63% |
| New member: "meet [named person]" in the first days | ~59% |
| "How did your intro with [name] go?" (3 options) | ~56% |
| Plain welcome message | ~47% |
| "Here are people you could meet" (a list of suggestions) | ~37% |
| Follow-up on those suggestions | ~29% |
| Generic event reminder | ~28% |
| One-off broadcast to members never contacted before | ~18% |

What that means, rule by rule:

- **Rules 1 and 2.** A named person plus a yes/no decision won. Being wanted ("someone
  asked for you", ~63%) beat being offered ("people you could meet", ~37%). Named-person
  asks got over three times the replies of broadcasts (~18%).
- **Rule 3.** The three-option outcome check drew ~56%. A follow-up on a list of
  suggestions, with no single decision in it, drew ~29%.
- **Rule 4.** The lapse notice, a change the member didn't cause, was the best-performing
  message of all (~65%).
- **Rule 5.** A welcome built around one named person (~59%) beat a plain welcome (~47%).
  About half of new members became active in their first month.
- **Rule 9.** Broadcasts cannibalise: a one-off broadcast used up attention that better,
  personal touches needed. That is why proactive touches are capped at about one per
  member per week, at least ~72 hours apart.
- **Rules 10 and 11.** Of ~200 intro requests over ~10 weeks, ~64% got a yes from the
  person asked, ~30% lapsed with no answer and ~6% were declined. Of those who answered a
  follow-up, about half said they hadn't actually connected. Net, roughly 1 request in 3
  became a real conversation, and people who did meet rated it about 4.7 out of 5. The
  first-move instruction and the follow-up timetable exist to turn yeses into
  conversations and to find out which ones happened.
- **Event messages.** At a party-style event, a named person to find, sent ~90 minutes
  before start, drew ~37% replies against ~28% for a generic reminder. But only about 30%
  of pre-matched pairs had both people actually turn up. Registration is not attendance,
  so every event message names a backup.
- **Short response windows.** People who had never engaged lapsed about two-thirds of the
  time, against about a quarter for members who had engaged before. Keep a runner-up
  ready.

Cite these as "in one ~850-member professional network". Never name the community and
never present the figures as an industry benchmark.

## Templates

Every template is under 500 characters and asks one question. Replace every
[square-bracket] variable or cut the phrase around it.

### Introductions (double opt-in)

**1. Consent ask** (to the person being asked; send this first, before anything about
them is shared with the requester)

```text
Hi [target first name], [requester full name], [requester role] at [requester org], asked to meet you. [One line: what they're working on and why you in particular.] Would you be happy for me to introduce you? Yes or no is fine, and no is completely OK. I haven't shared your details with anyone.
```

Onboarding variant, to an existing member: open with "[new member full name] has just
joined [community name]: [role] at [org]." and ask "Would you be up for a short hello in
the next couple of weeks?"

**2. Requester update** (after the consent ask goes out; don't name the person until
they say yes)

```text
Hi [requester first name], I've asked someone who fits well: [one line on who, without naming them, e.g. "a founder who has raised a seed round in climate"]. I'll come back to you by [date], and if it doesn't work out I'll suggest someone else. Is [date] soon enough, or is this time-sensitive?
```

**3. Connection message, chat** (after both say yes; a group chat with both, or the same
text to each)

```text
[Requester first name], meet [target first name], [target role] at [target org]. [Target first name], meet [requester first name], [requester role] at [requester org]. You've both said yes, so over to you: [one-line reason]. [Requester first name], could you message [target first name] with a couple of times that work, ideally within a day?
```

**4. Connection message, email** (after both say yes; both people in To:)

```text
Subject: Intro: [requester first name] <> [target first name], [short topic]

Hi both,

[Target first name], meet [requester first name] ([requester role], [requester org]), who [what they're working on or looking for].

[Requester first name], meet [target first name] ([target role], [target org]), who [relevant experience].

You've both said yes. [Requester first name], could you reply with a few times that suit, ideally within a day? I'll step back from here.

[Manager name]
```

**5. Lapse notice** (to the requester, when the window closes with no answer or after a
decline; don't name the next person yet)

```text
Hi [requester first name], I didn't hear back on the intro I asked about, so I've closed it. People get busy; it isn't a reflection on you. I have another good fit in mind: [one line on who, without naming them]. Want me to ask them?
```

After a decline, swap the first two sentences for "It isn't going to work out with the
person I asked this time." Never say "declined" or name who said no.

**6. Follow-through check, about 3 days after the intro** (to the requester, or both)

```text
Hi [first name], quick check on your intro with [other person first name]: have you two found a time to talk yet?
```

**7. Outcome check, about 10 days after the intro**

```text
Hi [first name], how did your conversation with [other person first name] go? Reply 1, 2 or 3:
1. Super valuable
2. It was OK
3. We didn't connect
Anything you add helps me make better intros.
```

**8. Recheck, about 7 days after "not yet" or "didn't connect"** (the last one; then stop)

```text
Hi [first name], one last check from me: did you and [other person first name] manage to talk in the end? Reply 1, 2 or 3:
1. Yes, and it was useful
2. Yes, it was OK
3. No, it didn't happen
If it didn't, I'm happy to suggest someone else.
```

### Onboarding

**9. Welcome built around a named intro** (send once the named member has said yes to
meeting them)

```text
Welcome to [community name], [first name]. You mentioned [what they're looking for or working on]. [Named member full name], [role] at [org], [one line: what they've done that fits], and would be glad to meet you. Shall I connect you two?
```

**10. Day 3**

```text
Hi [first name], did you and [named member first name] manage to find a time to talk?
```

If they've already met, use this instead:

```text
Hi [first name], one thing coming up that fits what you're after: [event name] on [date], [one-line reason]. Want the link?
```

**11. Day 14**

```text
Hi [first name], two weeks in: is there anyone or anything you're looking for right now that I could help with? One line is plenty.
```

**12. Day 30**

```text
Hi [first name], it's been a month since you joined [community name]. How is it working for you so far? Reply 1, 2 or 3:
1. Already useful
2. Mixed so far
3. Not yet
If it's 2 or 3, tell me what you hoped for and I'll find the right person.
```

### Re-engagement

**13. Someone asked for you** (only when a real member asked, or said yes when you
offered them the intro; no "we miss you", no recap of what they missed)

```text
Hi [first name], [requester full name], [role] at [org], would like to meet you: [one-line reason, e.g. they're building their first sales team and you've done it twice]. Happy for me to introduce you? Yes or no is fine.
```

**14. Profile ask** (for members never contacted, or whose profile is too thin to match;
only if they've opted into the community's communications)

```text
Hi [first name], it's [manager first name] from [community name]. I make introductions between members and I'd like to find you a useful one. What's one thing you're working on or looking for at the moment?
```

### Events

**15. Before the event: find [name]** (60 to 90 minutes before start, backup named)

```text
Hi [first name], one person to find at [event name] today: [match full name], [role] at [org]. [One-line reason.] If you can't spot them, try [backup full name] ([backup org]): [short reason]. Will you look out for [match first name]?
```

**16. Morning after**

```text
Hi [first name], thanks for coming to [event name]. Did you find [match first name]? Reply 1, 2 or 3:
1. Yes, and it was useful
2. Yes, briefly
3. We didn't meet
If you met someone you'd like to follow up with, tell me who and I'll connect you.
```
